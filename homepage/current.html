The place itself

What's here

Ten acres, and six places worth walking to.

Rare Fruit TrailA frost-free pocket growing things that shouldn't survive in Manatee County — star fruit, dragon fruit, jackfruit, lychee.
Butterfly GardenNectar and host plants chosen to pull butterflies through the season. Best on a warm, still morning.
Serenity GardenBenches, soft planting and a pond just beyond. The place to slow down mid-walk.
Rare Palms & PlumeriaSpecimen palms you won't find in a typical yard, and plumeria that perfume the path in summer.
Art in the ParkThe indoor gallery at the Galleria — rotating shows from local artists, and a cool place to pause.
Hidden Garden NurseryVolunteer-run, near the back gate. Plants propagated from the collection. Oct–May, Tue & Sat, 9am–noon.

Right now in the park

What's blooming, what's fruiting, what's flying — and what somebody just planted. Open any card for a few things worth knowing about it.

In bloom

Beautyberry

Callicarpa americana

This is the best time of year to see Beautyberry in its full purple. Like a Dr Seuss plant!

Near Office and around Cypress Pond

Jacob F. – High School Volunteer · CC-BY-NC · iNaturalist
A few things worth knowing

Around the beginning of September, tight clusters of vivid purple fruit appear directly around the stems. At Palma Sola, that is when Beautyberry suddenly seems to be everywhere.

American Beautyberry is a Florida native. Ours grow around Cypress Pond and the office, and some appear to have seeded themselves into new spots.

The fruit is edible, though fresh Beautyberries are tart and not especially flavorful; they are more often made into jelly, with a lot of sugar.

Heliconia

Heliconia psittacorum

Great shot of these blooming by Alaina, one of our high school volunteers! The mass planting outside the office is at times unruly, so we enjoy them now while we can.

Near Office

Alaina C. – High School Volunteer · CC-BY-NC · iNaturalist
A few things worth knowing

The showy orange bracts are not flowers — they are modified leaves. The actual flowers are the small structures tucked inside them. The bracts last for weeks after the true flowers have finished.

The curved bract shape is a near-exact match for a hummingbird bill. Plant and bird have been shaping each other through coevolution. In its native range, hummingbirds become territorial over specific heliconia plants, defending them as a private nectar supply.

Spotted

Northern Cardinal

Cardinalis cardinalis

Unexpected Helping Hand. Photographer Helen Lewis spotted something remarkable: a Northern Cardinal feeding a young Northern Mockingbird chick. A wonderful little moment of unexpected care, caught right here at the park.

Rare Fruit

Helen Lewis · CC-BY-NC · iNaturalist
A few things worth knowing

Males are bright red with a pointed crest and black face mask; females are warm buff-brown with red tinges. Both carry the same stout orange-red bill.

Both sexes sing. In many North American songbirds only the male does.

That heavy conical bill is a seed-cracker, opening tough seeds many birds cannot manage.

Spotted

Eastern Lubber

Romalea microptera

Jenny caught this beast as she walked to the Galleria and took this great shot of its coloring. Beautiful and creepy at the same time.

Office Decking

Jenny Gunter · CC-BY-NC · iNaturalist
A few things worth knowing

One of Florida's largest grasshoppers, an adult Eastern Lubber can reach about three inches long.

The wings are too small for sustained flight. Adults mostly walk and climb, with only short hops when disturbed.

The black, yellow, orange or red coloration is a warning. Lubbers contain defensive chemicals and can release a foul-smelling froth when threatened.

In bloom

Golden Shower Tree

Cassia fistula

To the left of the pavilion, between the butterfly garden and the rare fruit area is this hidden gem. Full glory of yellow right now!

Near Butterfly Garden

Rob Carr · CC-BY-NC · iNaturalist
A few things worth knowing

Long chains of yellow flowers hang from the branches in late spring and early summer, briefly turning an otherwise inconspicuous tree into something much easier to spot.

The dark cylindrical pods that follow can reach about two feet long and stay on the tree through the winter.

Break open a mature pod and it isn't one long hollow tube: the seeds sit in a row, separated by partitions, with dark pulp packed around them.

The Geiger Tree can be found in a couple spots. In the middle of the hibiscus garden in the field behind the office. Or in the welcome island. You passed in on the way in.

A few things worth knowing

Clusters of orange-red flowers can appear again and again through the warm season.

Rub a leaf and you have the other name for it — sandpaper leaf. Both surfaces are genuinely rough.

Pale, wrinkled fruits follow the flowers.

Newly planted

Rainbow Eucalyptus

Eucalyptus deglupta

Our team planted a new tree in September to replace the fallen baobab from the storms of past years. We also lost a rainbow eucalyptus near the cactus garden, so this is a welcome back of sorts!

Front Pond

Franky McArthur · CC-BY-NC · iNaturalist
A few things worth knowing

The bark is not dyed or treated. Every color on that trunk follows a predictable sequence: the outer bark peels away to reveal bright green inner bark, which then shifts through blue and purple, deepens into orange and red, and finally fades to brown — at which point the cycle starts over. Different patches are always at different stages, which is why the whole trunk reads as a mosaic.

A park built by its community

Palma Sola Botanical Park exists because local people decided a former county nursery was worth saving. It has run as a non-profit since 1993 with no federal, state or local government subsidy — volunteers do nearly everything else.

Recently: featured in Horticulture Magazine and on Bay News 9.
